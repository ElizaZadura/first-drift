Repetition itself can be useful for reinforcing information—think of it as practicing something over again until it's memorized perfectly—or creating rhythm. However, excessive repetition without variation may indeed lead into distortion—in this context meaning loss or degradation in quality:

1. **Data Analysis:** If a dataset contains redundant entries that are repeated excessively (say more than 90% identical), filtering out such repetitions can avoid skewing the results.

2. **Music Theory/Education:** Over-repetitions of musical motifs without variation might make them lose their interest, becoming monotonous and hence "distorted" in terms of aesthetic value or emotional impact on an audience/musician learning it (often referred to as 'tired repetition').

3. **Speech Processing/Speech Recognition Systems:** Excessive repetitions can confuse models if not handled properly; they might start perceiving them differently than intended words, leading toward distortion rather than understanding the context correctly—a situation called "overfitting."

4. **Signal Processing (Audio):** Repetitive signals that are too frequent or loud compared to surrounding frequencies may cause auditory masking—making it hard for listeners to distinguish subtle variations in other parts of a signal.

In each case, there is an optimal balance between useful repetition and distortion through over-repetition; identifying this point requires domain-specific knowledge.
