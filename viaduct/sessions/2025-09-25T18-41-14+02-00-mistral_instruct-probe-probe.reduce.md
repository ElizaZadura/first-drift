 In the context of refuse reduction, it might seem like a contradiction to "let contradiction stand." However, if we interpret "let contradiction stand" as acknowledging and accepting that there may be conflicting ideas or approaches when it comes to refuse reduction, then it can actually contribute to finding effective solutions.

For instance, one approach might focus on reducing the amount of waste produced at the source (e.g., promoting reusable containers, encouraging recycling), while another might focus on improving waste management practices in disposal sites (e.g., efficient sorting, energy recovery, minimizing greenhouse gas emissions). By acknowledging these conflicting approaches and finding ways to integrate them into a comprehensive refuse reduction strategy, we can more effectively address the complex challenges of waste management.

In this sense, "letting contradiction stand" means embracing diverse perspectives and strategies in our efforts to reduce refuse and promote sustainability.
