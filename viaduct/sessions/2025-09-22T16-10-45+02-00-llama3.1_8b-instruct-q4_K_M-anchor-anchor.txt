These phrases seem to be related to meditation and mindfulness practices. Here's a breakdown of what they might mean:

1. **"Reset posture: Presence, not performance."**

This phrase suggests that when you're meditating or engaging in mindfulness exercises, focus on being present in the moment rather than striving for some kind of ideal performance. Your goal isn't to achieve a certain state or feeling but to simply be where you are without judgment. The "reset posture" implies readjusting your attitude and approach to let go of any preconceptions about how the meditation should feel.

2. **"Hold silence if no form appears."**

This phrase can be interpreted in several ways depending on the context:

   - In a meditative sense, it might mean that if you're focusing on your breath or visualization (the "form") and it's not coming easily or at all, don't force it. Instead, focus on being silent or still within yourself. This can help to calm your mind and bring you back into the present moment.
   
   - In a more literal sense in spiritual practices like tai chi or qigong, it might refer to holding a meditation posture (like sitting or standing with specific alignment) without engaging in any movement or action if no inner energy or visualization arises.

Both phrases emphasize the importance of being present and letting go of expectations. They encourage you to approach your practice with an open mind and body, ready to adapt and be where you are without striving for a specific outcome.
