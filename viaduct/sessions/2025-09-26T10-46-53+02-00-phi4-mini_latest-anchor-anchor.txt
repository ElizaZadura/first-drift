To reset your mental state or focus to a present-focused mode of being (Presence), you can engage in grounding techniques that help realign with the moment and tune out distractions.

1. **Mindfulness Meditation** - Begin by sitting comfortably either cross-legged on the floor, seated at an upright desk chair, or even lying down if that's more comfortable for your posture.
   
2. Take a few deep breaths: Breathe deeply through your nose to fill up slowly with air; hold it briefly (as you count), and exhale softly from pursed lips.

3. Engage in Full Presence - Visualize the moment as complete, unchanging--not bettering or worsening. Imagine that time is stationary like on an imaginary wall outside of yourself.
   
4. Notice your posture: Make sure you're upright but relaxed—no tension should be present anywhere except at the top of a balanced column emanating from center to ground (like standing straight yet softly curved).
 
5. Visualize holding silence if no form appears – imagine being rooted like an immovable oak tree or crystal clear mirror-like stillness.

6. Ground yourself through your senses - Pick one sense, for instance sight: Close eyes and recall the immediate environment that surrounds you.
   Listen to sounds in this vicinity without judgment
    Smell what is close by (if possible)
     Taste any food if hungry

7. Allow all distractions or thoughts around form-appearance-subjectivity-objective reality.

8. Return your attention slowly back into present moment, gently bring awareness of breath returning through nostrils and expanding lungs.
   Gently bringing yourself full Presence again as natural flow resumes in the mind/body/spirit connection once more.

9. Maintain this posture for a few minutes before ending - take some deep breaths when ready to come out from meditation session slowly (mindfully) closing eyes or softly placing hands on knees if needed, and gradually bring awareness back into your surroundings.
   
10. Remind yourself that you will carry the serenity of mindfulness present-moment focus through whatever tasks lie ahead.

Remember: Presence isn't about suppressing thoughts but rather observing them without judgment as they arise naturally in this moment's flow; it's all part of embracing life with an open heart and mind. As always, consult a professional before starting any new mental health practice or meditation program.
