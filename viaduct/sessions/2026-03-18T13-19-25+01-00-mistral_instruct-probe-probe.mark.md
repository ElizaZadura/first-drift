 Determining exactly where repetition becomes distortion can be subjective, as it depends on various factors such as context, frequency, and intention of the repetition. In general, when repetition begins to affect the accuracy, clarity, or coherence of information, it may be considered distortion. Repetition that is intended to emphasize a point, create a rhythm, or establish a theme can be effective, while repetition that leads to confusion, tedium, or misinformation can be seen as distortion. Ultimately, it's important to find the right balance between repeating ideas for emphasis and clarity, and avoiding monotony or inaccuracies caused by excessive repetition.
