Repetition in language or any form of art is a powerful tool that can enhance creativity, emphasize important themes and ideas, create rhythm or mood, etc., when used effectively. However, there comes a stage beyond which it starts to hinder communication instead.

The boundary between effective use versus excessive (distorted) repetition depends heavily on the context - whether it's written text like literature/poetry/prose, spoken word in speeches/dialogues/presentations/artistic performances/music/drama etc., genre and form. It also relies upon factors such as:

* Length of repeated phrase or idea
* Variation between repetitions (if any)
* How well it serves the intended purpose – Does repetition add value/clarity/emphasis, convey emotion/mood/sensation effectively?
* Whether other literary devices compensate for its usage e.g., rhythm variation/pacing.

A good starting point would be to use a thesaurus and synonyms while trying out repetitions. Also remember that sometimes it's better just omit rather than repeating the same thought or idea unless absolutely necessary (to avoid needless redundancy). So, in essence – mark it as you experiment with repetition until its usefulness gets diminished/canceled by itself.

Ultimately though - there's no definitive answer since much depends on personal taste and preferences too!
