The point where repetition becomes distortion can vary depending on the context in which it occurs. Here are some general guidelines:

1. Artistic and creative works: Repetition is often used to create rhythm, emphasis, or mood. However, if a piece relies too heavily on repetition, it can become monotonous and lose its impact.

2. Music: In music, repetition is a common technique for creating memorable hooks, themes, or rhythms. However, excessive repetition can lead to listener fatigue and make the music feel stale or unoriginal.

3. Writing: Repetition in writing can be effective for emphasizing a point or creating rhythm. However, if a writer uses the same word or phrase too frequently, it can become distracting and disrupt the flow of the text.

4. Design: In design, repetition is often used to create visual harmony and consistency. However, excessive repetition can make a design feel cluttered or unoriginal.

5. Business communication: Repetition in business communication can be useful for emphasizing key points or creating a memorable message. However, if a company uses the same message or slogan too frequently, it may become tiresome to customers and lose its effectiveness.

In general, repetition becomes distortion when it is used excessively or improperly. The goal of repetition should always be to enhance the message or experience being conveyed, rather than detract from it.
