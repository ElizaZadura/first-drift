A "bow", as in a bow of steel, can bend under pressure without breaking. This is an example from the field of materials science or structural engineering where some metals and alloys exhibit high ductility allowing them to deform plastically (bend) when subjected to stresses up to their yield point before they fracture.


Another more poetic answer could be "time". Time bends but does not break - it can stretch, dilate in contexts such as relativistic physics under gravity or speed close to that of light. However, time itself is a constant flow and isn't susceptible like physical materials; it's an abstract concept rather than something with physical properties.


Alternatively if the answer requires metaphorical bending without breaking:

"Humans". People are capable of change (bending) in many ways - intellectually through learning or adapting beliefs but they generally don't break from experiences, hardship can be overcome by resilience.
