 Repetition can become distortion when it's excessive or meaningless, and it starts to overshadow the original message or intent. In such cases, the repeated information loses its impact, effectiveness, or clarity, potentially leading to confusion or annoyance in the audience. However, it's important to note that repetition can also be beneficial when used strategically to reinforce key ideas, create familiarity, and aid memory retention. The point where repetition becomes distortion depends on various factors, such as context, frequency, and the nature of the information being repeated. It's a delicate balance between reinforcing important messages and avoiding unnecessary redundancy.
